Learn an optimal policy by repeatedly evaluating and improving the current policy.

We estimate \(Q(s,a)\), not \(V(s)\): choosing \(\arg\max_a Q(s,a)\) needs no model.

Monte Carlo control: the two steps

1. Policy evaluation. Fix \(\pi_k\), generate episodes, and estimate action values by averaging returns:

\[Q^{\pi_k}(s,a)\approx\frac{1}{N(s,a)}\sum_{i=1}^{N(s,a)}G^{(i)}(s,a)\]

2. Policy improvement. Choose the best estimated action:

\[\pi_{k+1}(s)\in\arg\max_a Q^{\pi_k}(s,a)\]

Repeat evaluation and improvement until the policy stops changing.

Control one episode at a time

Full evaluation is slow. Instead, improve after every episode:

  1. Start at \(s_0\) and follow the greedy policy \(\pi(s)=\arg\max_a Q(s,a)\)
  2. For each first visit of \((S_t,A_t)\): \(\;N\leftarrow N+1,\;\; Q\leftarrow Q+\frac{1}{N}\big(G_t-Q\big)\)
  3. The policy is now greedy in the new \(Q\) (ties broken \(\leftarrow,\downarrow,\rightarrow,\uparrow\))
Every episode starts at \(s_0\) and only takes greedy actions.

On-policy MC control

  1. Generate an episode using the current \(\varepsilon\)-greedy \(\pi\).
  2. Update \(Q(s,a)\) from first-visit returns.
  3. Improve \(\pi\) to be \(\varepsilon\)-greedy in \(Q\).

The same policy generates the data and is evaluated and improved.

On-policy control: pick \(\varepsilon\)

\(\varepsilon\)

Each \(\varepsilon\) keeps its own learner; switching does not reset it. \(\bar V\): average of \(V(s)\) over ice cells, computed with the hidden model for grading.

How performance varies with \(\varepsilon\)

Too little exploration gets stuck; too much wastes episodes on random moves. A small \(\varepsilon\) balances the two.

Off-policy Monte Carlo

Behavior \(b\): \(\varepsilon\)-greedy in \(Q\), explores and generates the episodes.
Target \(\pi(s)=\arg\max_a Q(s,a)\): greedy, the policy we want to learn.

\[\rho_{t:T-1}=\prod_{k=t}^{T-1}\frac{\pi(A_k\mid S_k)}{b(A_k\mid S_k)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{k=t}^{T-1}\frac{1}{b(A_k\mid S_k)} & \text{if every } A_k=\pi(S_k)\\[6pt] 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\]

Weighted importance sampling: average returns with weights \(W=\rho\)

\[Q(s,a)=\frac{\sum_i W_i\,G_i}{\sum_i W_i}\]

Off-policy MC control: the update

For each episode from \(b\), walk backwards from \(t=T-1\), starting with \(G=0,\ W=1\):

\[\begin{aligned}G&\leftarrow \gamma G+R_{t+1}\\ C(S_t,A_t)&\leftarrow C(S_t,A_t)+W\\ Q(S_t,A_t)&\leftarrow Q(S_t,A_t)+\frac{W}{C(S_t,A_t)}\big(G-Q(S_t,A_t)\big)\\ \pi(S_t)&\leftarrow \arg\max_a Q(S_t,a)\\ &\text{if } A_t\neq\pi(S_t)\text{: stop the episode}\\ W&\leftarrow \frac{W}{b(A_t\mid S_t)}\end{aligned}\]
\(C\) is the running sum of weights, so \(W/C\) makes \(Q\) a weighted average.
Once an action disagrees with \(\pi\), \(\rho=0\) for everything earlier, so we stop.

Off-policy, one episode step by step

\(b\): \(\varepsilon=0.2\)-greedy, so \(b=0.85\) for the greedy action and \(0.05\) otherwise. Demo episodes are kept short (≤ 9 steps).
